# 2026-11-28 — Publishing package

**Pillar:** Project Management
**Headline:** Your project was green for six weeks. Then it was red.
**Audience:** TPMs, delivery managers, engineering managers and founders who write or read weekly status reports

## Caption

Green for six weeks. Red in week seven.

Projects rarely turn red overnight. The status report just stopped showing which way they were moving.

A traffic-light status is a snapshot of today, and an opinion. Nobody wants to be the first to say amber, so every report reads "on track" until the date is already lost. By the time the colour changes, the options to recover are gone. Green on the outside, red on the inside: the watermelon project.

Change what you report. Three things, every week: the forecast finish date and how it moved since last week; evidence, meaning what was finished against what was planned (done or not done, never "80%"); and the one ask, the decision or unblock you need and from whom. Plot the forecast date week by week and the trend shows up long before the colour does.

Then make the colour a rule, not a mood. Green: the forecast is before the committed date with buffer to spare. Amber: the forecast is eating into the buffer, or has moved two weeks running. Red: the forecast is past the committed date. Agree those thresholds with stakeholders at kickoff, so amber becomes arithmetic rather than an admission, and make sure every amber or red arrives with an ask. And thank people for an early amber. Punish it once and you will only ever see green.

What does "green" mean on your status report: a number your stakeholders agreed to, or someone's feeling on Friday afternoon?

3. Change the question: report the trend, not the colour. Forecast: the date you expect to finish and how it moved since last week. Evidence: what was finished against what was planned, done or not done, not 80 percent. Ask: the one decision or unblock you need, and from whom.

An annotation at week 3 reads: the trend was visible here.
5. Illustrative example, same project in week six, two status reports. Before: Status green, integration 80 percent complete, on track. After: forecast 14 March, was 6 March three weeks ago, committed 15 March; slipped 8 days in three weeks with one day of buffer left; 3 of 7 planned stories done; cause: vendor sandbox access still pending; ask: sponsor to escalate access by Tuesday.
6. Make the colour a rule, not a mood. Green: forecast is before the committed date with buffer to spare. Amber: forecast is eating into the buffer or moved two weeks running. Red: forecast is past the committed date. Agree the thresholds with stakeholders at kickoff; then amber is arithmetic, not an admission, and every amber or red arrives with an ask.
7. What to remember. Direction: a colour tells you where you are, a forecast trend tells you where you are heading. Evidence: report done versus planned; 80 percent complete hides the hardest 20 percent. Culture: thank people for an early amber; punish it once and you will only ever see green.
