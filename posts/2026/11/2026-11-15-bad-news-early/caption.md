## Caption

Bad news doesn't age well. Deliver it while it's still small.

Most delays are known weeks before they are announced. We tell ourselves the team might still recover, that we need more data, that raising it now looks like weakness. So the risk sits in a status report marked amber, until one Friday it turns red and becomes a surprise.

Time doesn't make bad news smaller. It removes your options. Early, a slip can be absorbed: cut scope, add a phase, move a dependency, shift a date nobody has announced yet. Late, the only option left is the one nobody wanted, and the client also learns you knew.

Same slip, two conversations (illustrative):
Week 3 of 12: "A vendor API we depend on is two weeks late. Here are three ways to protect the launch. I recommend option B."
Week 11 of 12: "We won't make the launch date."
The first is a decision. The second is a crisis.

A four-part message that works:
1. What happened: one sentence of fact, no adjectives.
2. Impact: dates, scope, cost, with your confidence level.
3. Options: two or three, each with its trade-off.
4. The ask: the decision you need, and by when.

Make it normal: agree escalation triggers in advance, never let a risk stay amber for more than two reports without a decision, and thank people who raise problems early. Teams copy what gets rewarded.
