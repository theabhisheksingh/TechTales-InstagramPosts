## Caption

"Be more proactive" isn't feedback. It's a verdict.

Engineers can act on what they did. They can only argue with a label about who they are. And vague feedback is ambiguous: "be more proactive" could mean raise risks before standup, pick up tickets without being assigned, or talk to the client directly. The listener picks one, often the wrong one, and feels judged while doing it.

A simple fix is the Situation-Behaviour-Impact (SBI) model from the Center for Creative Leadership. Describe a moment, not a person. Situation: when and where it happened, specific enough to recall. Behaviour: what you saw or heard, something a camera could record. Impact: what it caused for you, the team or the client. Then ask for their side.

An illustrative rewrite. Before: "You need to be more proactive with stakeholders." After: "In last week's release, you knew the migration would slip on Monday. The client heard on Thursday, from their own team. They have asked to review every timeline we send." Now there is something specific to discuss, and to change.

Before your next 1:1, write it down. Name one moment; if you cannot, you are not ready to give the feedback. Strip the adjectives: "careless" becomes "two failing tests merged". State the impact you saw, not the motive you assume. Then ask, and listen. Give it within days rather than at review time, use it for good work too, and remember that impact is not intent.
