## Caption

AI made experienced developers 19% slower.

They believed it made them 20% faster.

That isn't a hot take. It's the result of a randomised study by METR. Sixteen experienced open-source developers worked on 246 real issues in codebases of more than a million lines, and each task was randomly assigned "AI allowed" or "AI not allowed."

Before the study, the developers expected a 24% speed-up. Afterwards, they believed they'd got 20%. The clock said they were 19% slower.

The 2026 follow-up is more interesting. With newer tools, early numbers point to a speed-up, but METR calls that very weak evidence. Many developers no longer wanted to work without AI at all, which made a fair comparison hard.

The lesson isn't "AI doesn't work." It's that feeling productive and being productive are different things, and leaders who plan on feelings get surprised.

If you're rolling AI out to a team:
→ Baseline your delivery metrics first
→ Compare like-for-like work
→ Track quality, not just speed
→ Treat "it feels faster" as a hypothesis
