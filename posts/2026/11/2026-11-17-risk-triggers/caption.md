## Caption

A risk without a trigger is just a worry.

Most risk registers are written once. At kickoff the team lists twenty risks and rates each one High, Medium or Low. Six weeks later one of them lands. It was on the list, rated Medium, owned by "the team", and nobody saw it moving, because nothing told them what to watch.

The fix is two extra columns. A trigger is an observable signal that the risk is starting: a date, a number, an event. A response is what you do the moment the trigger fires, agreed in advance. Without them, a signal has to be noticed in a status review, debated, then acted on. With them, the response starts the same day, and you keep the weeks you would otherwise lose.

Same risk, written twice (illustrative):
Before: "Vendor API delay · High · Owner: PM."
After: Trigger: no sandbox access by 5 Dec, or fewer than 2 of 4 endpoints live in it. Response: test against mocks, move integration testing one sprint, tell the sponsor that day. Owner: one named person.

Rewrite each risk as a card: IF <condition>, THEN <impact on date, cost, scope>. WATCH <signal + threshold>. OWNER <one name>. RESPONSE <first action>. APPROVED BY <who>. If you can't write the WATCH line, you don't understand the risk yet.

Ratings go stale; a trigger tells you when a risk is no longer hypothetical. Agree the response before you need it, when decisions are cheapest. In weekly reviews, check triggers, not ratings.
