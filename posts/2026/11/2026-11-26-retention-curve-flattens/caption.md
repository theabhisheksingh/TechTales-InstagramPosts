**Audience:** Founders, product managers, TPMs and engineering leaders deciding what to build next in an early-stage or new product

## Caption

Signups prove your marketing works. Retention proves your product does.

The chart in most board decks is total users, and it only goes up. A cumulative count includes everyone who ever signed up, so it rises even when most of them left last month. A launch, a discount or a viral post can triple signups in a week. None of that tells you whether the product solved a problem worth coming back for.

Cohort retention does. Group users by the week they started, then track how many repeat the core action (not "logged in") in week 1, 2, 3 and onward. Read the shape, not one number. A curve that decays toward zero is a leaky bucket. A curve that flattens means some group found lasting value. Where it flattens depends on the product and how often the problem occurs.

An illustrative B2B reporting tool: a big campaign tripled signups, but new cohorts never built a first report and retention fell to near zero. The team paused paid acquisition and rebuilt onboarding around one goal: first report in ten minutes. More users would only have filled the bucket faster.

This week: name your core action, match it to its natural frequency (daily for chat, weekly for planning, monthly for payroll), log it as an event with a user and a timestamp, and review the cohort chart before the roadmap. Fix retention before you buy growth, because acquisition multiplies whatever the curve already is. Then study the users who stayed. They are your market.

What is the core action for your product, and does your team actually track it?
