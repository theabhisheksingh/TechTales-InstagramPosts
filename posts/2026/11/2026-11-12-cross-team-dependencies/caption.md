## Caption

Your critical path runs through a backlog you don't own.

Week 1: "We'll need an auth change from Platform." Week 3: a ticket marked "ASAP." Week 6: "It's planned for next sprint." Week 9: the launch slips, and the retro says "dependency risk."

Nobody did anything wrong. The dependency was real from week one. It just wasn't managed until week nine.

A cross-team dependency is a request in someone else's queue. It competes with their roadmap, their incidents and their own deadlines. "ASAP" gives them nothing to plan with. What they need is what exactly you need, by when, why it matters, and what happens if it's late.

Five moves that keep it on track:
1. Map every external input in week one.
2. Ask for a need-by date, with the reason behind it.
3. Agree the interface first, so a contract and a stub let both teams build in parallel.
4. Name an owner on each side and check in weekly.
5. Escalate early, with options and trade-offs, not blame.

Keep one card per dependency: need-by date, latest safe date, fallback, owners, status. The fallback is what makes escalation calm.
