**Pillar:** Project Management (Delivery · Estimation)
**Headline:** Your estimate isn't a date. It's a probability.
**Audience:** TPMs, engineering managers, delivery leads, senior engineers who get asked "when will it be done?"

## Caption

"Six weeks" is the most dangerous thing you'll say in a planning meeting.

Not because it's wrong. Because you meant a guess and they heard a commitment.

Every estimate is really a range of outcomes. Work can finish a little early, but it can run very late, and a single number usually sits near the hopeful end. Early in a project, the real effort can be up to 4× higher or lower than the estimate. That range only narrows as decisions get made.

What works better:
→ Give a range with a confidence level (P50 / P80)
→ Name what drives the risk
→ Set a re-estimate checkpoint tied to a decision
→ Commit externally at P80, plan internally at P50

Stakeholders don't need certainty. They need to know how confident you are, and when that will change.

Does your org accept ranges, or does it only want one date?
